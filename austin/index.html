When Your System Fails in Austin

When your heat or air quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. That's why we keep scheduling flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Emergency calls typically see a technician within a day, and we prioritize no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

Common emergencies we handle include air conditioners blowing warm air, outdoor units that hum but won't start, furnaces that ignite and then shut down, breakers that trip every time the system runs, iced-over evaporator coils, clogged condensate drains backing up into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have stopped responding. Our trucks carry the capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings needed to finish most repairs in a single visit. We provide upfront pricing every time, so you know exactly what the work will cost before we begin.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. Those few minutes can prevent a much larger repair.

How Austin Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
